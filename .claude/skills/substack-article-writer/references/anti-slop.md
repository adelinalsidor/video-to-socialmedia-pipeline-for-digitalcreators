# Anti-Slop: Guide for Clean, Concrete Content

## Forbidden phrases

### Generic openers (cut completely)
- "In today's fast-paced world..."
- "In this day and age..."
- "It's no secret that..."
- "It's important to note that..."
- "Without a doubt..."
- "As we all know..."
- "It's a well-known fact that..."
- "In an ever-changing world..."
- "Let's be honest..."
- "We've all been there..."
- "Whether you're a beginner or a pro..."

### Empty adjectives and adverbs (replace with specifics)
- amazing / incredible → the actual result, described
- game-changing / revolutionary → what it does differently, concretely
- must-have / essential → why, with the consequence of skipping it
- flawless / perfect → the specific look or effect achieved
- effortless → the actual steps involved (don't pretend there are none)
- unlock / elevate / unleash → the plain verb for what actually happens
- gorgeous / stunning (used as filler, not describing a specific result) → what makes it look that way

### False transitions
- "But here's the thing..."
- "And that's not all!"
- "Surprisingly (or not)..."
- "Here's a little secret..."
- "Ready for this?"
- "Now for the fun part..."

### Cliché conclusions
- "At the end of the day..."
- "In conclusion..."
- "The bottom line is..."
- "So there you have it!"
- "Don't wait — start today!"

## Positive rules

### R1: Every sentence earns its place
- If a sentence could be deleted without losing content, delete it.
- Test: read the sentence alone. Does it say something specific? If not, rewrite or cut it.

### R2: Replace adjectives with specifics
- NOT: "This technique gives you a flawless finish."
- YES: "This technique keeps concealer from creasing into fine lines by midday — which is the actual complaint most women over 40 have with it."

### R3: Ground every claim in the transcript, not in generic beauty-blog wisdom
This skill has no external research step — the only source is what the client actually said and demonstrated in the video. Every technique, product, or claim in the article must trace back to `transcript.md`.
- NOT: "Experts agree that priming your skin is key." (invented authority)
- YES: "The client uses a hydrating primer first — not to make foundation last longer, but because it stops powder from clinging to dry patches, which is what actually ages a look."
- If the client doesn't name a specific product or brand in the transcript, don't invent one. Refer to it descriptively ("a cream-based concealer," "a small fluffy brush") instead of guessing a name.

### R4: Be specific, not generic
- NOT: "Many women struggle with this."
- YES: "If you've ever caught your concealer settling into fine lines by 2pm, this is why."

### R5: First paragraph = immediate value
- Readers decide in 3 seconds. The opening should correct a real misconception or make a concrete promise — pulled directly from how the client opens the topic in the transcript.

### R6: Natural tone
- Write like you're explaining it to a friend over coffee, not presenting at a conference.
- Avoid stacked passive constructions: "It can be observed that..." → "You'll notice..."
- Sentence rhythm: alternate short (5-10 words) with medium (15-20 words). Avoid 30+ word sentences.

## Before/after examples

### Example 1: Slop intro → clean

**SLOP:**
> In today's fast-paced world, makeup routines have become more important than ever. It's no secret that finding the right technique can be a game-changer for how confident you feel every single day.

**CLEAN:**
> The biggest mistake isn't using too little concealer — it's putting it on before your skin is ready for it. If you've noticed your under-eye concealer creasing by lunchtime, that's almost always the real reason.

**Why it's better:** Opens with the actual myth-busting pattern the client uses, makes a specific claim, sets up a real problem instead of vague enthusiasm.

### Example 2: Vague description → concrete

**SLOP:**
> This amazing technique gives you an effortless, flawless glow that will completely transform your everyday look.

**CLEAN:**
> The client blends the cream blush with their fingers, not a brush — the warmth of your hands melts it just enough to sit into skin instead of sitting on top of it, which is what makes it look like your own color instead of "blush."

**Why it's better:** Names the actual technique, explains the mechanism, ties it to a real visual outcome instead of adjectives.

### Example 3: Generic CTA → warm and specific

**SLOP:**
> Don't wait! Subscribe now and unlock exclusive beauty secrets you won't find anywhere else!

**CLEAN:**
> If this is the kind of thing that's useful to you — real technique, explained simply, no filler — I write one of these every week. Come along.

**Why it's better:** Matches the warm, non-aggressive CTA style from `tone/stil-scris-client.md` instead of manufactured urgency.
